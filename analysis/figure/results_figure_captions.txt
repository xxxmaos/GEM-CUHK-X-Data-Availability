fig2_correction_benefits_harms
Benefits and harms of baseline-anchored correction on 1,046 clips. (a) Beneficial corrections, harmful revisions, and net corrections relative to the numerical baseline. (b) Paired transitions when replacing unprotected debate with the rule guard: 53 incorrect predictions become correct, while 23 correct predictions become incorrect. Failed protocols retain their prescribed numerical fallback. The reference method differs between panels.

fig3_paired_differences
Paired accuracy differences and subject-level variability. (a) Point estimates and 95\% subject-clustered bootstrap intervals (2,000 resamples; seed 42). Each difference is the first named method minus the second; direct and reinspection comparators in protected comparisons also use the rule guard. (b) The corresponding differences for each of six subjects, with sample counts. Colors and symbols identify the same comparisons in both panels. Only debate and reinspection are matched at six logical calls; direct arbitration uses three. Intervals are descriptive for this exploratory cohort and do not remove selection bias.

fig4_input_robustness
Performance under missing, degraded, and mismatched inputs. Each condition contains 1,046 clips. (a) Accuracy under modality removal and synthetic cross-clip IR replacement. (b) Accuracy as agent observations are increasingly blurred. Blur affects only the observation branch, leaving numerical probabilities unchanged. Synthetic IR mismatch is not physical temporal misalignment. Protection mitigates severe-blur damage but remains below the numerical baseline.

fig5_selective_prediction
Classification quality and selective prediction. (a) Risk--coverage curves and AURC computed from a shared numerical-confidence ranking, with ties resolved by sample key. Curves do not measure calibration of agent probabilities. (b) Outcome decomposition over all 1,046 clips; numbers within bars are clip counts. Both methods accept the same 770 clips (73.61\% coverage). Correct accepted outputs increase by a net 50, without changing the acceptance set. Rejected outcomes refer to internal predictions.

figS1_cohort_distribution
Distribution of clips across 40 activity classes and six subjects in the 1,046-clip cohort. Activity class IDs follow the prediction-label vocabulary. The class imbalance motivates reporting macro-F1 alongside accuracy.

figS2_guard_tradeoffs
Beneficial corrections versus harmful revisions, each relative to the numerical baseline. Points compare the full rule guard, three single-condition removals, and the learned guard. Higher correction counts and lower harm counts are desirable, but individual constraints trade off both quantities.

figS3_class_recall
Class-specific recall changes for the rule guard relative to the numerical baseline, with sample counts below. Differences are in percentage points. Large changes in classes with very few clips, particularly class 25 with three clips, should not be interpreted as stable class-level effects.
